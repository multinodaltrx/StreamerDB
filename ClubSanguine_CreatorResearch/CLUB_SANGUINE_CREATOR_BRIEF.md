# Club Sanguine — Creator Research Brief

## 1. What Club Sanguine Is

**Club Sanguine** is a stylized first-person deduction, management, and dark-comedy horror game.

The player works as a supernatural nightclub bouncer responsible for deciding who is allowed into the club.

Guests may include ordinary humans, vampires, thralls, monsters, suspicious patrons, and other supernatural or deceptive characters.

The player examines guests using physical clues, behavior, dialogue, and specialized tools before deciding whether to:

* admit them
* send them elsewhere
* hold them for further investigation
* banish/reject them
* take other context-dependent actions

Wrong decisions have consequences.

The game mixes deduction, management, horror, absurd situations, character interactions, and escalating nightclub chaos.

---

# 2. Core Player Fantasy

The easiest shorthand is:

> **Papers, Please meets supernatural nightclub bouncer.**

The player repeatedly encounters unfamiliar characters and has to answer:

> "What the hell is this person, and should I let them inside?"

The fun comes from imperfect information, observation, decision-making, mistakes, consequences, and unusual character reactions.

---

# 3. Tone

Club Sanguine is not intended to be grim realistic horror.

Its tone combines:

* gothic horror
* pulp horror
* dark comedy
* strange characters
* supernatural mystery
* absurd situations
* stylized violence
* occasional tension
* streamer-friendly failures and surprises

The visual direction is strongly stylized rather than realistic.

The game should appeal to players who enjoy strange indie games with a distinctive identity.

---

# 4. Why It Should Work Well for Creators

Club Sanguine naturally produces moments that work well in videos and livestreams:

* confidently making the wrong deduction
* suspicious guests behaving strangely
* hidden supernatural identities
* tool reactions
* unexpected consequences
* funny dialogue
* escalating mistakes
* arguing with chat about whether somebody is human
* recurring characters
* bizarre patrons
* memorable character designs
* management decisions
* risk/reward choices
* "I knew it!" moments
* "How was I supposed to know that?" moments

Personality-driven creators are particularly relevant because the game gives them repeated opportunities to react, speculate, joke, and make decisions.

---

# 5. Creator Audience We Want

We are primarily looking for creators whose existing viewers already demonstrate interest in one or more of:

* deduction games
* inspection games
* checkpoint / gatekeeper games
* unusual job simulators
* indie horror
* comedy horror
* management games
* games built around identifying suspicious people
* games involving moral or procedural decisions
* supernatural mysteries
* quirky indie games
* games that generate strong streamer reactions
* short or medium-length indie experiences that creators frequently showcase

Genre overlap matters more than superficial gothic aesthetics.

Someone who repeatedly plays deduction/inspection games is generally more relevant than someone who only plays vampire-themed games.

# 10. Target Creator Size

We are deliberately interested in the long and middle tail of creators.

## YouTube

Preferred rough range:

**1,000–500,000 subscribers**

This is not an absolute rule.

Smaller creators may qualify if their audience fit is exceptionally strong.

Larger creators may qualify if there is unusually strong evidence of direct compatibility.

---

## Twitch

Preferred rough range:

**5–1,000 average concurrent viewers**

Again, audience fit matters more than the exact number.

---

# 11. Size Is Not the Main Ranking Signal

Do NOT equate popularity with suitability.

Example:

A creator with 18,000 subscribers who has played:

* Papers, Please
* That's Not My Neighbor
* Contraband Police
* Buckshot Roulette

may be substantially more valuable to us than a generic 2-million-subscriber gaming channel.

We are searching for **audience compatibility**, not fame.

---

# 12. Language

For the initial campaign:

**Prioritize English-language creators.**

A creator does not necessarily need to live in an English-speaking country.

Their primary content simply needs to be reasonably usable for an English-language marketing campaign.

Other languages may be researched in a later campaign.

---

# 13. Activity

Prefer creators who have published or streamed within approximately the last:

**90 days**

Creators inactive for longer periods should generally receive lower priority.

Exceptions can be made for unusually strong fits.

Record the latest activity date whenever reasonably verifiable.

---

# 15. Public Contact Information

For eventual outreach, record legitimate PUBLIC contact channels when available.

Examples:

* public business email
* creator business page
* management contact
* website contact form
* publicly provided social profile
* publicly advertised Discord/community contact

Never attempt to uncover hidden/private email addresses or other private contact information.

If no public contact method exists, use:

---

# 16. Evidence Standard

We do not want speculative creator matches.

Whenever possible, every creator should have direct evidence demonstrating why they belong in the database.

Good evidence includes:

* YouTube video covering a comparable game
* playlist covering a comparable game
* Twitch VOD
* Twitch category/history evidence
* creator's own public page showing relevant content
* multiple relevant videos

Store direct URLs.

A creator with three strong evidence URLs is preferable to one whose compatibility is inferred from a generic description.

Never fabricate evidence.

---

# 17. Useful Creator Content Types

Relevant formats include:

* Let's Plays

* edited gameplay
* indie-game showcases
* first impressions
* horror videos
* reaction-heavy gameplay
* variety gaming

Channels consisting mainly of news, trailers, aggregation, or stolen/reuploaded content are poor targets.

---

# 18. Strong Creator Signals

Increase priority when a creator:

* covered several Tier 1 games
* covered both Tier 1 and Tier 2 games
* frequently discovers smaller indie games
* has an active personality/commentary-driven format
* regularly experiments with unusual games
* publishes recently
* has a reachable public business contact
* has an audience large enough to matter but small enough that indie outreach is realistic
* returns repeatedly to deduction or horror-adjacent indie titles

---

# 19. Weak Creator Signals

Reduce priority when a creator:

* played only one loosely related horror game
* primarily covers AAA games
* primarily covers competitive games
* hasn't uploaded recently
* creates generic gaming-news content
* uploads trailers rather than original gameplay
* primarily republishes clips
* has little evidence of playing indie games
* appears in search results only because of metadata rather than relevant content

---

# 20. Search Philosophy

Do NOT begin with generic searches like:

`indie horror streamer`

Instead use actual games as audience fingerprints.

Examples:

* `"Papers, Please" gameplay`
* `"That's Not My Neighbor" gameplay`
* `"Contraband Police" gameplay`
* `"Not Tonight" let's play`
* `"Quarantine Zone" streamer`
* `"I'm on Observation Duty" gameplay`
* `"Buckshot Roulette" creator`

Then inspect creators and look for overlap between their content libraries.

A creator appearing across several relevant game searches is a strong signal.

---

# 21. Network Expansion

Research should gradually behave like a graph.

Example:

Papers, Please
↓
Creators who covered it
↓
Other games those creators covered
↓
Repeated relevant game
↓
New search seed
↓
Additional creators
↓
Creator overlap

The objective is to uncover the relevant creator ecosystem, not merely scrape the first few pages of a search engine.

---

# 22. Initial Scale Goal

The eventual goal may be thousands of candidates.

A rough long-term funnel is:

**8,000–12,000 discovered candidates**

↓

**~3,000 meaningfully qualified creators**

↓

**~300–500 strong manual outreach prospects**

These are directional targets rather than strict quotas.

Quality and evidence integrity are more important than hitting exact counts.

---

# 23. Batch Research

Do not attempt the entire dataset in one run.

Work in batches of roughly:

**100–200 candidates**

After each batch:

* save results
* deduplicate
* validate
* score
* identify which searches worked
* continue into underexplored areas

---

# 24. Scoring

Final `fit_score` is out of 100.

Component maximums:

* Direct comparable coverage: **30**
* Genre fit: **20**
* Recent activity: **15**
* Audience-size suitability: **10**
* Multiple comparable games: **10**
* Contactability: **5**
* Personality / streamer suitability: **5**
* Indie-game affinity: **5**

Total:

**100**

The research agent may assess component values based on evidence, but the final arithmetic and tier assignment should be performed by the deterministic repository script.

---

# 25. Tiers

* **S: 85–100** — exceptional fit
* **A: 70–84** — strong prospect
* **B: 55–69** — worthwhile prospect
* **C: 40–54** — weaker / experimental
* **Below 40** — long tail

---

# 26. High Priority Outreach

A creator can be flagged:

`HIGH PRIORITY`

when several of these are true:

* multiple direct comparable games
* currently active
* strong indie-game affinity
* realistic audience size
* commentary/personality-driven content
* legitimate public contact channel
* evidence that they regularly try games from unknown/small developers

---

# 27. Data Integrity

Never fabricate:

* subscriber counts
* viewer counts
* country
* language
* emails
* game coverage
* upload dates
* contact information
* URLs

If reliable information cannot be found, record:

`Unknown`

Unknown data is better than invented data.

---

# 28. Deduplication

The same creator may operate on several platforms.

Do not automatically create separate creator records because they have both Twitch and YouTube.

Preferred identity hierarchy:

1. YouTube channel ID
2. Twitch username
3. canonical profile URL
4. normalized creator name + corroborating social links

Cross-platform accounts should be merged when evidence strongly establishes that they belong to the same creator.

---

# 29. Research vs Outreach

THIS REPOSITORY IS CURRENTLY IN RESEARCH MODE.

The research agent must NOT:

* send email
* send DMs
* message Discord users
* post to social networks
* submit contact forms
* request Steam keys on somebody's behalf
* impersonate the developer
* begin automated marketing

It may only discover and record publicly available contact routes.

Outreach will be a separate explicitly authorized phase after human review.

---

# 30. Primary Objective

Build a defensible, evidence-backed database answering:

> **Which active creators already have an audience likely to enjoy Club Sanguine?**

The best candidate is not necessarily the largest creator.

The best candidate is the creator whose existing content provides the strongest evidence that **Club Sanguine naturally belongs on their channel.**
